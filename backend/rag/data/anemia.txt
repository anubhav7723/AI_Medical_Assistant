ANEMIA

Definition:
Anemia is a condition where the hemoglobin level is lower than normal, reducing oxygen transport in the body.

Normal Ranges:
Hemoglobin:
- Men: 13–17 g/dL
- Women: 12–15 g/dL

RBC:
- 4.5–5.9 million cells/mcL (men)
- 4.1–5.1 million cells/mcL (women)

Hematocrit:
- Men: 40–50%
- Women: 36–44%

Key Parameters:
Hemoglobin, RBC, Hematocrit, MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW

Additional Indicators:
WBC, Platelets, Neutrophils, Lymphocytes, PDW, PCT, BMI

Symptoms:
Fatigue, weakness, pale skin, dizziness, shortness of breath

Causes:
Iron deficiency, blood loss, chronic disease, vitamin deficiency

ML Feature Insights:
- Low Hemoglobin → strong indicator of anemia
- Low MCV → microcytic anemia
- High RDW → variation in RBC size
- Low RBC → reduced oxygen capacity

Lifestyle & Diet:
Iron-rich foods: spinach, lentils, red meat
Vitamin B12 and folate intake recommended

User Questions:
Q: Why is my hemoglobin low?
A: Low hemoglobin indicates reduced oxygen-carrying capacity, often due to iron deficiency.

Q: What should I eat to improve anemia?
A: Include iron-rich foods and vitamin C for better absorption.

Q: Is anemia dangerous?
A: Moderate to severe anemia can affect heart and brain function if untreated.
